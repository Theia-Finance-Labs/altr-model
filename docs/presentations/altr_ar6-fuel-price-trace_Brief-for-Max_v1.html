AR6 Fuel-Price Trace

Two tasks in ar6_scenario_workflow. One is a ten-minute label fix. The other is a trace of where the carbon tax gets into the fuel price — it decides whether the ALTR model is charging carbon twice.

Task 1 — fix the pathway unit label

Owner: you · Size: one line · Status: confirmed by Bertrand's trace, nothing to investigate

In pipeline/combined_pipeline.py, pathway_unit is set to MWh/yr whenever secondary energy exists, and a few lines later every power row with capacity has scenario_pathway overwritten with capacity_mw (commit a93e2e1, "change scenario_pathway to be in capacity"). The value is capacity in MW; the label still says generation. Make the label say MW for those rows (or rename the column). The values are fine — the model already treats them as capacity.

Task 2 — find where the carbon tax gets into fuel_price

Owner: you for the trace; the fix goes to whoever the trace points at (you if the pipeline adds it, Tron/Bertrand if it is the raw AR6 definition) · Size: a couple of hours · Status: open, priority

The symptom. In the policy scenarios, fuel_price for gas is not the fuel price — it is the fuel price plus the carbon tax. WITCH 5.0, China, USD per MWh of fuel:

Gas matches "baseline plus carbon" in every year. Coal matches in 2030 and reverts to the baseline price from 2040 — inconsistent within one provider, which is a lead in itself. Share of 2035+ region-years where the target fuel price is more than 1.5× the baseline:

Why it matters. The model's input contract says prices are ex-carbon, and the model charges carbon itself: carbon_cost_net = Q × carbon_price × EF. Wherever the fuel price already carries the tax, a plant pays carbon twice. On the reference run that is 7.3 tn of extra fuel cost next to 9.8 tn of explicit carbon, against a headline risk number of −4.0 tn. Every absolute number the model has produced on this extract is inflated by it.

What to establish, in order

  1. Which AR6 variable feeds fuel_price, per provider. Expected Price|Primary Energy|Coal / Gas / Oil / Biomass (US$2010/GJ × 3.6 → USD/MWh). Check ar6_variables_with_mapping.csv and the conversion in pipeline/combined_pipeline.py.
  2. Whether the workflow adds carbon anywhere — a join on Price|Carbon, a "consumer price" step, or the gap-fill in pipeline/step4_gapfill_simple.py (it fills prices; confirm it never builds a carbon-inclusive one).
  3. If the workflow is clean, whether the raw AR6 rows are carbon-inclusive for WITCH, IMAGE and REMIND. Pull the raw values for WITCH 5.0 / EN_NPi2020_500 / CHN / coal for 2030 and 2040 and compare the source variable — the 2030-only jump points at either a per-year variable switch or a model-side reporting convention.
  4. Write down the answer and the proposed contract: fuel_price ex-carbon in every scenario. Two candidate fixes — carry the baseline scenario's fuel-price path into the target, or subtract carbon price × fuel emission factor for the carbon-inclusive providers. Say which and why. If it is a pipeline step, fix it; if it is the raw definition, the subtraction goes to Tron/Bertrand on the marts side.

import pandas as pd
sc = pd.read_csv("ar6_scenarios_20260901.csv")      # the 2026-09-01 extract, 337,192 rows

# baseline vs target fuel price, WITCH China, gas and coal
w = sc[(sc.scenario_provider == "WITCH 5.0") & (sc.scenario_geography == "CHN")
       & sc.scenario.isin(["EN_NoPolicy", "EN_NPi2020_500"])
       & sc.technology.isin(["GasCap - w/o CCS", "CoalCap - w/o CCS"])
       & sc.scenario_year.isin([2030, 2040, 2050])]
print(w.pivot_table(index=["technology", "scenario_year"], columns="scenario",
                    values=["fuel_price", "carbon_price_usd_per_tco2"]))

# share of 2035+ region-years with target fuel price > 1.5x baseline, per provider
keys = ["scenario_provider", "scenario_geography", "scenario_year", "technology"]
th = sc[sc.technology.isin(["CoalCap - w/o CCS", "GasCap - w/o CCS"]) & (sc.scenario_year >= 2035)]
base = th[th.scenario.str.contains("NoPolicy|INDCi2100")][keys + ["fuel_price"]].rename(columns={"fuel_price": "fb"})
targ = th[th.scenario.str.contains("NPi2020_500|PkBudg900|2Deg")][keys + ["fuel_price"]].rename(columns={"fuel_price": "ft"})
m = base.merge(targ, on=keys); m = m[m.fb > 0]
print(m.assign(hi=m.ft > 1.5 * m.fb).groupby(["scenario_provider", "technology"]).hi.mean())
